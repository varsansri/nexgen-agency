Choosing the right development methodology can make or break your project. Here is a practical guide to agile vs waterfall and when to use each.

### Waterfall: Traditional, Sequential

Waterfall follows a linear progression: Requirements, Design, Implementation, Testing, Deployment, Maintenance.

**Best for:**
- Fixed-scope projects with clear, unchanging requirements
- Regulatory or compliance-heavy industries (healthcare, finance)
- Projects with external dependencies on fixed timelines
- Smaller, well-understood projects

**Drawbacks:**
- Inflexible to changing requirements
- Testing only happens at the end
- Client does not see working software until late stages

### Agile: Iterative, Collaborative

Agile breaks work into 1-4 week sprints, delivering working increments regularly. Scrum and Kanban are the most common frameworks.

**Best for:**
- Projects with evolving requirements
- Startups and innovation-driven projects
- Client-facing applications where user feedback shapes direction
- Complex projects benefiting from iterative improvement

**Drawbacks:**
- Can be harder to price upfront
- Requires active client involvement
- Scope creep needs active management

### Hybrid: The Best of Both Worlds

Many successful projects use a hybrid approach: waterfall for discovery and architecture, agile for development and iteration. This combines upfront planning with flexible execution.

### Which Should You Choose?

| Situation | Recommended Approach |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Fixed budget, known scope | Waterfall |
| Evolving product, startup | Agile (Scrum) |
| Enterprise with compliance needs | Hybrid |
| Small marketing site | Waterfall or Kanban |
| Complex web application | Agile |
